Cut once.

Three steps. After the block, neither side has a private copy to edit.

01

Write

The payer writes who is paid, how much, and what it was for. One line, up to 140 bytes. An optional mark is the hash of a file or a post.

02

Sign

The wallet signs those bytes with a key that never leaves the browser. The notch is SHA-256 of the words. Change a letter and the signature dies.

03

Notch

A miner cuts the receipt into a block. The payee opens the same transaction and hashes the words again. The two halves match, or the receipt is false.

What the block holds.

Words

The line you can read

What the payment was for. Both wallets show it. The chain rejects a line break, so the receipt cannot hide a second version.

Notch

Hash of those words

Anyone can recompute it. The explorer does it in your browser and checks the signature against the payer’s address.

Mark

Optional hash of the work

A 32-byte hash of the report, the post, or the file. The file stays off-chain. The mark is what both sides compare.

Public

A receipt is meant to be shown

Amounts are visible. Hiding them would make the two halves impossible to hold up next to each other. That is the product.

From a receipt to a wage.

  1. Live

    Receipts

    Signed payments with words, a notch and an optional mark. Wallet, explorer, faucet and open mining.

  2. Next

    Streams

    A wage that notches every few blocks. What is already earned stays with the payee when the stream stops.

  3. Then

    Errands

    Coins locked to a job. The worker submits a mark, the payer releases them, or the deadline returns the coins.

  4. After that

    Mainnet

    Genesis once streams and errands are specified and tested. $TALLY on Robinhood Chain carries the work there. tTALLY does not.

Questions.

Is tTALLY the same as $TALLY?

No. tTALLY is the testnet coin. The faucet drips it and miners earn it. It has no market. $TALLY is the Robinhood Chain token that funds the path to mainnet. Its contract is published at launch.

Where are my keys?

In this browser. The wallet makes a 32-byte seed on your device and derives an ed25519 key from it. The node never sees the seed. The seed is the only way back in.

What if the payee is offline?

The receipt does not need them. It sits in the block. When they open it, their wallet hashes the same words and shows the same notch.

Can I run a node?

Yes. One Node.js program is the node, the explorer and the wallet. Clone the repository, install, and start. You hold the chain yourself.
